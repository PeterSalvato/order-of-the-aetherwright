The sigil is the whole system drawn as one mark. It is a seal: it marks a document, a tool or a work as belonging to the Order. It records nothing. Recording is the work of the Codex.

## Construction

A ring holds a star drawn as one unbroken line through eight points. Each point carries one domain, and the domain's glyph is set beside it, outside the ring. The III and the æ stand inside the ring. Chevrons at the sides mark phase and are no part of the star. The sigil as drawn on the cover of this tract is the canonical form.

## Positions

| Point | Glyph | Domain |
|-------|-------|--------|
| Top, left | `◀` | Intuition |
| Top, right | `▶` | Illustration |
| Upper left | `■` | Design |
| Upper right | `◆` | Craft |
| Lower left | `⬟` | Photography |
| Lower right | `⬢` | Code |
| Bottom, left | `▼` | Narrative |
| Bottom, right | `▲` | Systems |

Followed from the top, the line meets the points in this order: `◀ ▼ ■ ⬢ ▶ ▲ ◆ ⬟`, and returns to `◀`.

## The sigil of a work

Every work has its own sigil, drawn from the canonical one. Only the glyphs of the domains the work involved are set beside the ring, and only part of the star is drawn: the shortest stretch of its line that passes through every one of those points. A work that involved all eight domains carries the whole star.

The sigil of a work shows which domains it involved and nothing more. The order of the work and how it ended are recorded in its Codex.

## Use

The sigil is set on covers, seals and stamps, and at the head of a document. It is never required.
